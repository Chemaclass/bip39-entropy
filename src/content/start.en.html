The short version

  • A seed phrase is a number written as words, and whoever knows the number owns the coins.
  • Its strength was decided the instant it was created, so the process behind it is your only evidence.
  • Let a hardware wallet generate it, or throw dice to check every bit yourself, and never pick the words yourself.
  • Write them on paper by hand, restore from that paper before you fund the wallet, and never photograph or type them.

Before anything else

This page never asks for a recovery phrase, and no legitimate tool ever will. Not a wallet maker, not an exchange, not a support agent, not a “validator”. When someone asks for your words, the request is the attack.

Nothing here has a field that takes a phrase, and nothing here talks to a network. Read the source, or pull the cable, and check.

01 In one minute

A seed phrase is a number written as words. Twelve words are a 128-bit number plus four bits of checksum that catch typing mistakes. Each word is an entry in a public list of 2048, the same list in every wallet and the same list printed on the poster.

Whoever knows that number owns the coins, and nothing else counts. Not the person holding the device, not the person whose name is on anything. There is no account to recover and no support desk that can step in.

So one thing stands between your coins and everyone else, which is that nobody can guess the number. Whether that holds was decided the instant the phrase was created, before you wrote anything down.

02 Where the number comes from

A phrase generated properly is one of 2^128 possibilities, and nothing reaches that. An attacker doesn't need to break any mathematics, only to search a set smaller than you thought you were using. That has already emptied wallets, through generators seeded from the clock and through passphrases people invented, and in every case the phrase looked ordinary. You can't look at twelve words and tell.

A hardware wallet is the right answer for almost everyone, trading an unauditable chip for a phrase that never crosses a screen. Choosing words yourself has no defence at all, because twelve words picked from thirty-two you find memorable is sixty bits, and a serious hardware budget works through sixty bits.

03 A recipe you can follow

The first is what most people should do, the second is for anyone who wants to watch every bit of their own entropy arrive, and in both the restore test comes before the money.

Recipe A · A hardware wallet. The default.

  1. 1 · Buy it from the maker, or a reseller they list. A box that arrives with a phrase already inside is a trap, and so is a phrase handed to you by anyone at all.
  2. 2 · Check it before it holds anything, running the maker's authenticity check and reading the firmware version off the device's screen rather than the computer's.
  3. 3 · Let the device generate the phrase. The words appear on its screen, and should never appear on your computer at any point.
  4. 4 · Write them down by hand, numbered one to twelve, spelled exactly as shown, clear enough for a stranger to read. When the device asks you to confirm, read them back off the paper rather than from memory.
  5. 5 · Restore before you fund it. Wipe the device and restore from your written copy alone. This is the step people skip, and it's the one that matters.
  6. 6 · Send a small amount first. Receive a little, then spend some, so you know both directions work before you move the rest.

Recipe B · Dice and this poster. Slower, auditable end to end. The roller takes the throws and prints a worksheet for a table with no computer on it.

  1. 1 · Count the throws. Twelve words is 128 bits, or 64 accepted throws of one die, which discards bring to about 96.
  2. 2 · Throw and record, in order, on fresh paper with no phone on the table. Read 1 2 3 4 as 00 01 10 11 and throw a 5 or 6 again, which is what keeps the four outcomes equally likely. Never “fix” a throw you don't like.
  3. 3 · Cut the bits into words. Each group of eleven bits is an index from 0 to 2047, and on the poster the high bits pick the column while the low bits pick the row.
  4. 4 · Let a machine finish the last word, because the twelfth mixes your bits with four checksum bits you can't compute by hand. It learns your entropy either way, and the point is that it did not choose it.
  5. 5 · Restore, then burn the worksheet, which until then is a second copy of your seed in longhand. Then the small amount first, as in Recipe A.

04 Offline, and staying that way

Most people should stop at Recipe A and let a hardware wallet do the work, and that is the recommendation rather than a hedge. The rest of this is for anyone generating entropy by hand or about to print.

An air gap is a fact about hardware, not a setting, because settings get changed by software, updates and sleep cycles. Unplug the cable, then disable wifi and bluetooth separately, since a paired phone is a route to the internet.

Your phone is never offline in the sense that matters, because it synchronises and indexes its own contents, so a photograph taken in airplane mode uploads itself the moment the connection returns. A live session booted from a USB stick is the honest way to get a clean machine.

Printing is the point of this project, and a printer is a computer you have never thought of as one. That costs you nothing here, because the poster is the public BIP-39 word list and the blank worksheet holds no results. The dangerous sheet is the one you filled in, so write that by hand and never print it.

05 After you have the words

Restore before you fund. Both recipes said it, and here it is a third time, because a backup you have never restored is a guess about the future. The moment to find out that a word is unreadable is while the wallet is empty.

Paper or steel. Handwritten paper costs nothing, though fire and water are the two things most likely to happen to a house, and stamped metal survives both for an hour of work. Store it somewhere dry and private, and not beside the device.

Four things never to do, all the same mistake in different clothes. Never photograph it. Never type it anywhere except the wallet you are restoring on purpose, never store it in a password manager or a cloud note, and never split it in a scheme you invented.

The first three turn a local secret into a remote one, and the fourth leaves about sixty-two bits in each half. Home-made splits lose coins far more often than they prevent theft.

The passphrase and multisignature are worked through on Protect: what each buys, what it costs, how each fails. Read it before you add either, because a layer you have not rehearsed is a way to lose coins rather than keep them.
